Wellness Resources

Practical guides for the moments that feel hard — breathing, studying, sleeping, and finding real human help.

🫁 Box Breathing (2 min)

  1. Sit comfortably, unclench your jaw, drop your shoulders.
  2. Breathe in through your nose for 4 counts.
  3. Hold for 4 counts.
  4. Breathe out slowly through your mouth for 4 counts.
  5. Hold empty for 4 counts. Repeat 4 rounds.

🌿 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding

When thoughts race, anchor yourself in your senses. Slowly name:

  • 5 things you can see
  • 4 things you can hear
  • 3 things you can touch
  • 2 things you can smell
  • 1 thing you can taste

Used widely for acute anxiety — it pulls attention back to the present moment.

💆 60-Second Body Reset

  1. Roll your shoulders back 5 times, then forward 5 times.
  2. Tilt your head ear-to-shoulder, hold 10 seconds each side.
  3. Unclench your jaw — let your tongue rest off the roof of your mouth.
  4. Shake out your hands like flicking off water, 10 seconds.
  5. One slow breath in, one long sigh out. Done.

🌅 5-Minute Morning Calm

  1. Before touching your phone: 5 slow breaths.
  2. Stretch arms overhead, 20 seconds.
  3. Drink a full glass of water.
  4. Write today's one most important task on paper.
  5. Step into daylight (or open a window) for a minute.

📚 Beating Exam Anxiety

  • Shrink the syllabus: list topics, rank by weightage, start with high-value ones.
  • Active recall: close the book and write what you remember — far stronger than re-reading.
  • Pomodoro: 25 min focus + 5 min break. After 4 rounds, take 20 minutes.
  • Mock under time: one timed practice paper removes most exam-day surprise.
  • The night before: light revision only, then sleep. Cramming all night backfires.

⏰ Time Management Basics

  • Tonight: time-block tomorrow — study, meals, rest, sleep.
  • Eat the frog: hardest task first, when your mind is freshest.
  • Two-minute rule: if it takes under 2 minutes, do it now.
  • Weekly review: 15 minutes every Sunday to plan the week.
  • Protect one rest day (or half-day) — burnout destroys more marks than a day off.

Move first

A 10-min walk before studying boosts focus.

😴 Sleep Hygiene Guide

  • Fixed timing: same sleep & wake time daily — even weekends.
  • Wind down 30 min: dim lights, no phone, light stretching or reading.
  • Cool, dark room; avoid caffeine after 4 PM.
  • 4-7-8 breathing in bed: in 4, hold 7, out 8 — 4 rounds.
  • Can't sleep after 20 min? Get up, do something calm in dim light, return when drowsy.

🔋 Rest Without Guilt

Rest is not laziness — it's when your brain consolidates learning and repairs stress damage. Signs you need real rest, not more coffee:

  • Reading the same line three times
  • Irritability over small things
  • Headaches, heavy eyes, shallow breathing

Take a 20-minute nap (set an alarm) or a proper evening off. You'll return sharper.

🎓 Finding a Counselor

  • Campus counseling cell: most colleges offer free, confidential sessions — check your student portal or notice board.
  • Ask a trusted teacher or mentor for a referral; they've guided students through this before.
  • What to expect: a private conversation, no judgment, no forced advice — you set the pace.
  • Cost worry? Government hospitals and NGOs (like iCall) offer low-cost or free support.

💬 How to Help a Friend

  • Listen first. "I'm here. Tell me what's going on" beats instant advice.
  • Don't minimize: avoid "just relax" — try "that sounds really tough."
  • Stay with them if they mention self-harm; help them call a helpline.
  • Follow up the next day. Being remembered matters enormously.
